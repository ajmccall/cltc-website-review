01 / Calm · established · understated

Classic club

A polished, traditional club feel without looking old-fashioned.

The look
Forest green and ivory, elegant serif headings, fine borders and an arched court photograph.
The layout
A balanced text-and-photo opening, a quiet facts strip, an editorial three-column play section and an airy gallery.
Best if we want…
A timeless, reassuring identity with the information and club character doing the talking.
Trade-off
More restrained: less immediate sporting energy than the modern option.

02 / Bold · energetic · photo-led

Modern tennis

A confident, sportier homepage that puts being on court first.

The look
Deep green, tennis-ball lime, bold sans-serif headings and a full-width photographic opening.
The layout
An immersive hero, dark facts band, numbered play cards, a feature-image gallery and a high-contrast newsletter panel.
Best if we want…
A strong first impression and a contemporary identity centred on playing, improving and joining.
Trade-off
More assertive. The image overlay needs care to keep text readable, and strong photography matters.

03 / Warm · welcoming · sociable

Community club

An approachable local club, with life off court part of the story.

The look
Warm cream, soft green and peach, rounded panels, friendly typography and a tennis-and-clubhouse photo collage.
The layout
A welcoming split opening, colourful facts tiles, wider two-column play cards and softer coaching and junior panels.
Best if we want…
Beginners, families and social players to feel that there is a place for them here.
Trade-off
A softer sporting identity. Competitive tennis needs to remain prominent further down the page.

04 / Expressive · editorial · unexpected

Courtside magazine

A local sporting institution, presented like an independent magazine.

The look
Ink and vermilion, oversized mixed typography, panoramic photography and strong editorial rules.
The layout
A headline-led opening, numbered activity rows, stripped-back coach profiles and pricing. Far fewer boxed cards.
Best if we want…
A confident, distinctive identity that reflects the club’s character and life beyond the courts.
Trade-off
Less conventional as a tennis website. Needs good photography and disciplined writing to stay sharp.

05 / Playful · graphic · full of life

Clubhouse noticeboard

A lively wall of club posters, translated into a usable website.

The look
Cobalt, lilac, lemon and coral. Off-centre photography, hard-edged poster blocks and solid offset shadows.
The layout
Coaching, social-play and membership shortcuts, colourful activity posters, a framed coaching area and a pinned-photo gallery.
Best if we want…
A people-led club with a sense of fun, where beginners and families feel welcome from the first screen.
Trade-off
More visually busy and a bigger departure from the existing identity. Practical information must remain easy to find.

Where we are now

The foundations are in place.

The provisional homepage already covers the club, ways to play, teams, coaching, the weekly programme, gallery, fees, newsletters and contacts. It has clear joining routes and visible coach contacts.

The main design opportunity is to reduce the repetitive card feel, give sections a clearer visual hierarchy, and use more club photography. All five proposals keep joining and coaching prominent. The two wildcards explore a bigger departure in headline style, colour and layout.

Before launch: confirm fees, weekly sessions, team details and the latest newsletter. The existing LTA badge says 2024/25; obtain a current badge rather than changing its date in the design.

How to review

Pick a direction, not every detail.

  1. Open each option on a laptop and a phone. Look beyond the first screen.
  2. Which one feels most like CLTC?
  3. Would a new member easily find coaching, fees and how to join?
  4. Does it balance juniors, social play and competitive tennis?
  5. Choose a favourite, then name one thing to keep and one thing to change.
